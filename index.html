A love that gives

What does God's love
look like in our lives?

In the Christian story, God's love is a gift: steadfast, generous, and offered to every person. We see it in the beauty of creation, in people who care for us, and in the hope to begin again.

We don't have to earn love by being perfect. We can receive it with gratitude—and let it shape how we treat ourselves, our neighbours, and the world around us.

Stories to carry with us

12 stories.
Big-hearted lessons.

The Bible is a library of stories, poetry, wisdom, and letters. Here are 12 well-known stories from the Old and New Testaments, each with a lesson to think about.

✳Genesis 1–2

01 · The beginning

Creation

The Bible opens with a poem about God creating the heavens, earth, animals, and people, and calling creation good.

Carry it with you: Treat the natural world and every person as precious.

⌂Genesis 6–9

02 · A promise and a new beginning

Noah and the Ark

Noah builds an ark to protect his family and animals from a great flood. Afterward, the rainbow is a sign of God's promise.

Carry it with you: Keep hope, care for living things, and remember the promises we make.

✦Genesis 12; 15; 21

03 · Trust and a family

Abraham and Sarah

Abraham and Sarah leave their home to journey to a new land. Their story follows a long wait and God's promise of a family.

Carry it with you: Trust and patience can help us through uncertain times.

≈Exodus 3; 12–14

04 · Freedom and courage

Moses and the Exodus

Moses leads the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt. In the story, they cross the sea and begin a journey toward freedom.

Carry it with you: Stand up for people who are treated unfairly and seek freedom for all.

✧1 Samuel 17

05 · Bravery beyond size

David and Goliath

Young David faces the giant warrior Goliath with courage, a sling, and trust in God, even when others doubt him.

Carry it with you: Courage can come from standing up for what is right, not from being the strongest.

☼Daniel 6

06 · Faith under pressure

Daniel in the lions' den

Daniel continues to pray even when it is forbidden. He is thrown into a den of lions and, in the story, is kept safe.

Carry it with you: Hold on to your values and care for people who feel afraid.

⌁Jonah 1–4

07 · Second chances

Jonah and the great fish

Jonah tries to run away from a difficult task and is swallowed by a great fish. He gets another chance to go to Nineveh.

Carry it with you: We can change direction, try again, and make room for others to change too.

☆Luke 2:1–20

08 · A child is born

The birth of Jesus

Jesus is born in Bethlehem and laid in a manger. Shepherds visit after hearing the news from angels.

Carry it with you: Welcome each person with gentleness and joy.

⌁Luke 10:25–37

09 · Make room for a neighbour

The Good Samaritan

A traveller is hurt on the road. A Samaritan stops, tends his wounds, and makes sure he is cared for—showing that a neighbour is anyone who needs our compassion.

Carry it with you: Notice who might need a hand, especially someone outside your usual circle.

◌John 6:1–14

10 · Share what you have

Loaves and fishes

A child offers five loaves and two fish to a hungry crowd. Jesus gives thanks, shares the food, and there is enough for everyone—with baskets left over.

Carry it with you: A small gift can become something wonderful when we share it.

⌂Luke 15:11–32

11 · Welcome someone home

The lost son

A father welcomes his returning son with open arms. Jesus tells this story to show a love that forgives and celebrates a fresh start.

Carry it with you: Forgiveness can open the door to healing and new beginnings.

✝Luke 23–24

12 · Love and hope

The cross and the resurrection

The Gospels tell how Jesus was crucified and buried, and how his followers later found the tomb empty. Christians celebrate this as the resurrection of Jesus.

Carry it with you: In Christian faith, love and hope can endure even in the face of suffering.

Many traditions, shared world

A world of faiths.
A chance to understand.

People around the world find meaning in many different religions and spiritual traditions. Each is diverse, and no short list can capture them all. Learning with respect helps us live well together.

Christianity

Many churches and traditions

Christians follow the teachings of Jesus and the Bible. Many emphasize love of God and neighbour, forgiveness, and hope.

Islam

Muslim communities worldwide

Muslims worship one God, Allah, and follow the Qur’an and the example of Prophet Muhammad. Prayer, charity, fasting, and community are important practices.

Judaism

A people, faith, and tradition

Jewish life is shaped by the Torah, covenant, worship, community, and traditions passed through generations. Jewish people have diverse beliefs and practices.

Hindu traditions

Many paths and practices

Hindu traditions include diverse teachings about the sacred, dharma (living rightly), and paths such as devotion, knowledge, and action.

Buddhism

Rich schools and teachings

Buddhists follow the teachings of the Buddha. Many practise mindfulness, compassion, and wisdom on a path toward understanding and freedom from suffering.

Sikhism

Faith rooted in equality and service

Sikhs believe in one God and teachings of the Gurus. Equality, honest work, sharing with others, and serving the community are central values.

Bahá’í Faith

Unity of humanity and religions

Bahá’ís believe in one God and the unity of humanity. The faith encourages peace, justice, education, and the harmony of religions.

Jainism

Non-violence and care for life

Jains follow teachings that emphasize non-violence (ahimsa), self-discipline, and respect for all living beings.

Shinto

Japanese spiritual tradition

Shinto is a Japanese tradition connected with kami—sacred presences associated with nature and places—and with rituals, festivals, and community.

Daoism / Taoism

Chinese philosophical traditions

Daoist traditions explore living in harmony with the Dao, or “Way,” often valuing simplicity, balance, and connection with nature.

Zoroastrianism

Ancient faith with living communities

Zoroastrians follow the teachings of Zarathustra and honour the choice to support truth and goodness through good thoughts, words, and deeds.

Indigenous traditions

Many distinct peoples and ways

Indigenous peoples have many living spiritual traditions, often deeply connected to ancestors, community, land, and responsibilities to future generations.

And many more

Many paths and worldviews

There are countless other faiths and spiritual paths. Some people do not follow a religion; everyone deserves respect.

These traditions are not all the same, and people within each tradition have different beliefs and practices. There are also countless local, ancestral, and new religious movements around the world.
